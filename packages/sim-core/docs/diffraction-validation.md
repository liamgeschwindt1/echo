# Wedge diffraction: validation

Generated by `node scripts/sim-diffraction-validation.mjs` (2026-10-18). Design tool, not validation against measurements: the checks below compare the model with exact or independent numerical solutions of the same idealised problem.

## Model and references

- Rigid wedge, uniform theory of diffraction (UTD): Kouyoumjian-Pathak coefficient with the Fresnel transition function, spherical incidence, oblique incidence through `sin beta` and `L = s s' sin^2(beta) / (s + s')`. A 90 degree building corner is an exterior wedge with `n = 3/2`.
- Why UTD and not BTM in production: it is a closed-form complex frequency response per path, which the frequency-domain renderer, the reach map and the audit can all evaluate cheaply and identically. The Biot-Tolstoy-Medwin (BTM) solution is exact for an infinite rigid wedge and is used here, independently, as the reference.
- Formulas were not taken from memory alone: the coefficient is the four-term cot / F form; the BTM reference is the closed form for an infinite edge. They were cross-checked against each other numerically (the production coefficient and the independent BTM time-domain solution agree to 0.01 dB and 0.3 degrees for half-plane and 90 degree wedge geometries, see `test/sim-wedge.test.js`), and the BTM normalisation was fixed by the exact half-plane shadow-boundary value of one half (-6.02 dB).
- R. G. Kouyoumjian and P. H. Pathak, "A uniform geometrical theory of diffraction for an edge in a perfectly conducting surface", Proc. IEEE 62(11), 1448-1461, 1974.
- J. B. Keller, "Geometrical theory of diffraction", J. Opt. Soc. Am. 52(2), 116-130, 1962.
- M. A. Biot and I. Tolstoy, J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 29, 381-391, 1957; H. Medwin, J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 69, 1060-1064, 1981 (BTM, used as the independent exact reference).
- U. P. Svensson, R. I. Fred and J. Vanderkooy, "An analytic secondary source model of edge diffraction impulse responses", J. Acoust. Soc. Am. 106(5), 2331-2344, 1999.
- Double diffraction (edge to edge): product of the two coefficients with parallel-edge spreading `1/sqrt(s0 s1 s2 (s0+s1+s2))` and the distance parameters `L1 = s0 s1 / (s0 + s1) sin^2 b1`, `L2 = (s0 + s1) s2 / (s0 + s1 + s2) sin^2 b2`. Round the same building the connecting leg runs along a shared face, where each grazing end halves the coefficient (the standard UTD rule for incidence on a face). **This part is an engineering approximation and is not checked against an exact thick-screen solution.** Slope diffraction and vertex (corner point) diffraction are omitted.
- Reflection coefficients inside the wedge coefficient: walls use the simulator wall factor, roofs use 0 (roofs do not reflect in this simulator), so no roof-reflection boundary appears.
- Causality and smooth shadow boundary: see the sections below. Ground effects at edges and facade scattering are not modelled.

## 3. Causality

Impulse source (one sample), noise off, node behind a 10 x 10 x 15 m building, direct path blocked (order 2). Both the diffracted response and an ideal delayed impulse (free field, same fractional delay) are band limited at the 48 kHz render rate, so both ring before their arrival; the check is that the diffracted response does not ring more than the ideal impulse.

- Earliest path arrival `length / c`: sample 18986.60; peak of the rendered response at sample 18988.
- First sample above -20 dB of the peak: 18987 (arrival minus one sample = 18985); first above -40 dB: 18981.

| samples before the peak | 1 | 2 | 5 | 10 | 30 | 100 | 1000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| diffracted (dB re peak) | -2.71 | -27.96 | -36.44 | -42.47 | -51.61 | -59.69 | -67.17 |
| ideal delayed impulse (dB re peak) | -15.79 | -21.08 | -28.57 | -34.43 | -43.87 | -54.33 | -74.88 |

Requirement: the diffracted contribution does not arrive before `length / c` minus one sample. Measured: met at the -20 dB level; below -20 dB the pre-arrival level is band-limiting ringing of the sampled onset (the wedge impulse response has a 1/sqrt(t) onset), compared in the table with the ideal impulse above. The coefficient is evaluated down to 1 Hz: clamping it at 10 Hz was measured to add a -47 dB pre-arrival plateau, so the grid starts at 1 Hz.

## 8. Not done / limits

- Optional wave-solver check (`scripts/sim-wavesolver-check.mjs`) was not implemented.
- Double diffraction (including the grazing connecting leg along a face) is not validated against an exact solution.
- A single wedge cannot connect a source and receiver on opposite sides of a building, so at `diffraction_order: 1` such a receiver gets no diffracted path; order 2 is needed for over-the-roof and round-the-ends paths.
- Ground effects at edges, facade scattering, slope diffraction and vertex diffraction are not modelled.
