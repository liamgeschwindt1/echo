Limits of this simulation

  • Free field plus the ground and any buildings you add. Buildings are flat, axis-aligned concrete boxes with one placeholder absorption coefficient (0.03, not measured) for every surface. Only the vertical faces and the ground reflect; roofs block sound but do not reflect.
  • Diffraction is on by default (order 2). Each building edge (4 vertical corners, 4 roof edges) is a rigid 90° wedge and uses the uniform theory of diffraction (Kouyoumjian and Pathak 1974) with a complex, frequency-dependent coefficient, so the loss rises with frequency and the diffracted sound arrives no earlier than its path length divided by the speed of sound. The direct and diffracted fields join smoothly at the shadow boundary (about −6 dB on it). Order 1 is one edge; order 2 adds edge to edge (two edges, also round the same building and over its roof), reflection then edge, and edge then reflection. A single edge cannot connect two points on opposite sides of a building, so order 1 gives no path there. Weak paths (more than 50 dB below the strongest at 250 Hz, 1 kHz and 4 kHz) are pruned and listed in the audit. Double diffraction is an engineering approximation, not checked against an exact solution; slope and vertex diffraction are left out.
  • Not modelled: facade scattering, and ground effects at edges (the ground still reflects as before). Roofs and building undersides do not reflect. A bearing that follows a diffracted path points at the last edge it bent round (or the reflection point after it), not at the source, and the back-trace works in the horizontal plane only. The wave rings do not diffract; the reach map and the heatmap do. The diffraction constants are design values, not measurements.
  • With diffraction order 0, a building between a source and a node removes the direct path completely (a pessimistic worst case), and there is no transmission through walls. Real facades reflect less cleanly than flat boxes.
  • The wave rings are a loudness display, not true wavelengths: ring brightness follows the clip's 10 ms envelope with 1/r loss, and each ring is the 3D wavefront sphere cut by the slice plane, so rings from sources above or below the slice grow from a point. They do not diffract.
  • The heatmap (on by default) is an incoherent sum over arrivals (no interference). Pulse shows the level reaching each point at the playhead, peak-hold keeps the highest level so far, level is the clip mean (or peak). Diffraction uses the wedge model above, levels depend on the placeholder constants, and the wave rings are a stylised loudness display, not true wavelengths. It is computed for the slice height, from the same path finder as the render, and does not need Create.
  • The reach map and the side view are energetic (incoherent) sums over paths in three octave bands: interference is ignored, so a real microphone can measure several dB more or less, especially for tones. Same air absorption, bounce losses and diffraction as the audio render; the map uses its own reflection order setting (default 2).
  • Uniform air: no wind and no temperature gradients, so rays do not bend. Static sources.
  • Source levels, ground reflectivity and the SPL-to-dBFS constant are placeholders until confirmed.
  • Ambient noise is independent per mic, which is pessimistic for localisation.
  • Mic timing has no hardware jitter, clock drift or mismatch.
  • A flat-square node cannot tell above from below and assumes the source is below it.

Repeats and sweeps

Each seed re-renders every mic, so large runs take minutes. Fix rate is the share of sounds that got a position.

Save simulation

Nodes, sources, buildings, map area and settings are saved. Rendered audio is not: press Create after opening to hear it again. Sounds you uploaded yourself are not stored.
